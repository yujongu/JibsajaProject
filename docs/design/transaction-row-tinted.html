Five ways to tint a row

The tinted card is the direction. What's still open is how the tint is applied — a flat wash reads calmly but weakly, a full outline separates hard but adds eleven coloured rectangles to a screen. All five below tint the whole card; they differ in what carries the edge and where the category label sits. The strength control in the bar applies to every variant at once, so you can settle layout and intensity separately.

Reading the mockups

Three things to check before you pick

Dark mode needs more tint than light

A 9% wash over white is clearly visible; the same 9% over the #131D30 card is almost nothing. Each strength here sets a higher percentage in dark than in light (Medium is 9% / 15%) — otherwise every variant looks solved in light and unsolved in dark.

The trade rows still look flat

Buy uses primary #0055B2 and Deposit positive #059669 — both single constants with no dark variant, so their tint barely lifts off the navy card. Compare the Buy row against 여행 in the dark column at any strength. Whichever variant wins, those five type colours want light/dark pairs like the twelve categories already have.

Eleven tinted cards is the real test

Any of these looks good as a single row. Judge them as a column: scroll each frame and ask whether the list still feels like one surface or eleven stickers. That is where A and E separate from C and D.
