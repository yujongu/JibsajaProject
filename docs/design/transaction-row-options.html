Five ways to make a row say what it is

Today a row's category is carried entirely by hue and glyph — the words 식비, 배달음식, 교통 are never drawn on screen. Twelve categories map to twelve hues, so telling one from another means recognising a colour from memory. Below: the row as it ships now, then five designs that put the category back into words. Every design renders the same eleven rows, in both app themes, in the app's real palette.

Worth knowing before you pick

Three things these mockups exposed

Type colours have no dark variant

The twelve category hues ship as light/dark pairs, but the five type colours in _typeColor are single constants. Buy uses primary #0055B2 and Deposit uses positive #059669 — both sit dark on the #131D30 card. Look at the Buy and Deposit rows in the dark column: they read dimmer than every expense. Worth fixing whichever design wins.

Non-expense rows have no category

Only Expense rows carry a Category column, so Buy, Sell, Transfer, Deposit and unrecognised rows fill the same slot with their type. Every design above does this, which is what finally makes deposits and transfers scannable — today they're a badge in 11px type at the far right edge.

Amount sign is a separate fix

Amounts render in plain primary text in all six designs, exactly as they do today — money in and money out look identical. Colouring the sign (green in, neutral out) is orthogonal to the category question and can be added on top of whichever design you pick.
